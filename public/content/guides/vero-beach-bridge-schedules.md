## Navigating Vero Beach's Waterways: Your Essential Bridge Guide

Vero Beach, a jewel on Florida's Treasure Coast, is defined by its stunning barrier island and the vibrant Indian River Lagoon that separates it from the mainland. Connecting these two distinct halves are three crucial bridges, each playing a vital role in daily life, commerce, and recreation. For residents, visitors, and especially mariners, understanding these structures – their clearance heights, route numbers, and the ongoing 17th Street repair work – is key to a smooth and enjoyable experience.

This comprehensive guide from VeroTides.com delves into the specifics of the Barber Bridge (SR-60), the Wabasso Bridge (SR-510), and the 17th Street Bridge (SR-656). We'll cover vertical clearances, traffic tips and the repair project affecting the 17th Street Bridge. Whether you're a seasoned boater, an angler seeking the perfect spot, or simply curious about our local infrastructure, consider this your definitive resource.

### Good to Know First: These Are Fixed Bridges

All three Vero Beach crossings (Barber, Wabasso, 17th Street) are **fixed, high-level spans with about 65 feet of vertical clearance**, so there are no bridge openings, tender hails or opening schedules to plan around. Boats taller than 65 feet should not attempt the passage. The signals and habits below still apply to movable bridges elsewhere on the Intracoastal Waterway (for example in neighboring counties), and are good general practice around any bridge.

*   **VHF Radio:** Always monitor VHF Channel 16 and hail the bridge tender on Channel 13 when approaching a drawbridge. State your vessel's name, direction of travel, and request an opening.
*   **Sound Signals:**
    *   **One long blast followed by one short blast:** Request for opening.
    *   **Five short blasts:** Danger signal (used if the bridge tender doesn't respond or if there's an emergency).
*   **Bridge Tender Signals:**
    *   **One long blast:** Bridge will open.
    *   **Five short blasts:** Bridge will not open (due to schedule, malfunction, or emergency).
    *   **Red light:** Bridge is closed.
    *   **Green light:** Bridge is open or opening.
*   **No Wake Zones:** All bridges are typically surrounded by "No Wake" zones. Observe these strictly to prevent damage to other vessels, docks, and the sensitive shoreline, and to ensure safety for all.
*   **Currents:** Be acutely aware of strong currents, especially near Sebastian Inlet and during tidal changes, which can significantly impact your vessel's maneuverability when approaching a bridge.
*   **Patience:** Bridge tenders manage multiple requests and vehicular traffic. Be patient and maintain a safe distance from the bridge and other vessels while waiting.

### The Barber Bridge (SR-60 / Merrill P. Barber Bridge)

The Merrill P. Barber Bridge, commonly known as the Barber Bridge, is the iconic gateway to Vero Beach's barrier island, connecting the mainland via State Road 60 (20th Street) directly to Ocean Drive and the heart of our beachside community.

*   **Location:** Central Vero Beach, connecting 20th Street (mainland) to Beachland Road (barrier island).
    *   **Approximate Coordinates:** 27.6405° N, 80.3660° W
*   **Type:** High-level fixed span bridge. This is excellent news for mariners, as it means **no drawbridge operations!** You can pass underneath at any time, provided your vessel's height is within the clearance limits.
*   **Vertical Clearance:** The Barber Bridge offers a generous vertical clearance of **65 feet (19.8 meters)** at mean high water (MHW). This clearance accommodates most sailboats and larger vessels navigating the Indian River Lagoon.
*   **Significance:** Beyond its practical function, the Barber Bridge is a landmark. Its graceful arch provides stunning panoramic views of the lagoon, the city skyline, and the Atlantic Ocean. It's a popular spot for walkers, joggers, and cyclists.
*   **Boating & Fishing Tips:**
    *   **Navigation:** Due to its high clearance, navigation under the Barber Bridge is generally straightforward. Stay within the marked channel.
    *   **Currents:** While not as strong as near Sebastian Inlet, currents can still be noticeable, especially during strong tides.
    *   **Fishing:** The bridge pilings and surrounding structure can attract baitfish and larger predators. Anglers often target snook, redfish, and trout around the bridge. Be mindful of boat traffic and FWC regulations.
    *   **No Wake Zone:** Strictly observe the no-wake zone extending well beyond the bridge structure.

### The Wabasso Bridge (SR-510)

Heading north from central Vero Beach, the Wabasso Causeway Bridge is the northernmost Intracoastal crossing in Indian River County, linking the mainland community of Wabasso to the barrier island and Wabasso Beach.

*   **Route:** State Road 510 (Wabasso Road / Wabasso Causeway). SR-510 runs from US-1 to SR A1A; the county-maintained road west of US-1 is signed CR-510. The bridge itself is on SR-510. ([Wikipedia: Florida State Road 510](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_Road_510))
*   **Location:** Northern Indian River County, crossing the Indian River at Wabasso.
    *   **Approximate Coordinates:** 27.76° N, 80.42° W
*   **Type:** Fixed two-lane concrete bridge, completed in 1970 (it replaced an older 1927 drawbridge). **It is not a drawbridge and has no opening schedule.**
*   **Vertical Clearance:** About **65 feet (20 meters)**. ([Wikipedia: Wabasso Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wabasso_Bridge))
*   **Boating & Fishing Tips:**
    *   **Approach:** Stay in the marked channel and keep clear of other vessels near the bridge.
    *   **Currents:** Strong current can occur near the bridge during tidal changes; be prepared for swift water movement.
    *   **Spoil Islands:** Marshall Island sits between the two bridge spans, and several spoil islands nearby are popular for picnicking, fishing, and birdwatching.
    *   **Fishing:** The bridge structure and nearby channels are popular spots for snook, redfish, and other species.

### The 17th Street Bridge (SR-656 / Alma Lee Loy Bridge)

The Alma Lee Loy Bridge, commonly known as the 17th Street Bridge, is the southernmost of Vero Beach's three main crossings, connecting the mainland to the barrier island via 17th Street.

*   **Route:** State Road 656.
*   **Type:** Fixed concrete bridge, completed in 1979, four motor-vehicle lanes plus bike lanes. **It is not a drawbridge and has no opening schedule.**
*   **Vertical Clearance:** About **65 feet (20 meters)**. ([Wikipedia: Alma Lee Loy Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alma_Lee_Loy_Bridge))

#### Repair Project: Latest Status

FDOT District 4's SR 656 / 17th Street east end of bridge rehabilitation project began on September 5, 2023. The original estimate was summer 2028; FDOT later said work would finish by **fall 2026**, two years early ([Vero News, Oct. 2025](https://veronews.com/2025/10/17/span-tastic-17th-st-bridge-repair-completion-date-moved-up-again/)). As of **August 1, 2026 the project was roughly 98 percent complete**, FDOT said it would be done "by the end of summer," and drivers should still expect single-lane closures as needed until it wraps up ([Vero News, Aug. 13, 2026](http://veronews.com/2026/08/13/is-repair-work-on-17th-street-bridge-actually-dare-we-ask-almost-done/)). Check the FDOT District 4 project page and [FL511](https://fl511.com) for current closures.

*   **Project Scope:** Repair and replacement work on the eastern portion of the bridge (about the eastern 400 feet), concrete road surfaces, sealing, signage, and pavement markings. This is structural repair of a fixed bridge; it does not involve drawbridge machinery.
*   **Impact on Vehicular Traffic:** Expect lane closures, often at night while pavement markings are installed. Consider the Barber Bridge when you need a guaranteed open route, and allow extra time.
*   **Impact on Marine Traffic:** Watch for work barges, buoys, and construction equipment near the bridge, and check Coast Guard Local Notices to Mariners for any temporary waterway restrictions.

### General Tips for Navigating Vero Beach Bridges and the Indian River Lagoon

*   **Always Consult Current Charts:** NOAA nautical charts are your best friend. They provide up-to-date information on depths, channels, aids to navigation, and bridge clearances.
*   **Monitor Weather:** The weather in Florida can change rapidly. Always check forecasts before heading out and be prepared for sudden storms.
*   **Observe FWC Regulations:** Familiarize yourself with Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission (FWC) regulations regarding fishing, boating, and manatee protection zones. The Indian River Lagoon is a critical habitat for manatees, and "Slow Speed" or "Minimum Wake" zones are strictly enforced.
*   **Enjoy the Spoil Islands:** The Indian River Lagoon is dotted with numerous spoil islands, many of which are publicly accessible and offer fantastic opportunities for picnicking, camping, or simply enjoying nature. Be sure to practice Leave No Trace principles.
*   **Safety First:** Always ensure you have all required safety equipment on board (life jackets, fire extinguisher, sound-producing device, etc.). File a float plan with someone on shore.

### Conclusion: Bridging Connections in Paradise

The bridges of Vero Beach are more than just concrete and steel; they are lifelines that connect our communities, facilitate commerce, and open up endless recreational possibilities on the beautiful Indian River Lagoon. By understanding their operations, respecting their schedules, and exercising caution, both drivers and mariners can ensure safe and enjoyable passage.

Whether you're cruising under the majestic Barber Bridge, passing under the fixed spans of the Wabasso or 17th Street bridges, or simply admiring them from shore, these structures are an integral part of the Vero Beach experience. Stay informed, stay safe, and enjoy all that our unique coastal environment has to offer!
