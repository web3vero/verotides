## Navigating Vero Beach's Waterways: Your Essential Bridge Guide

Vero Beach, a jewel on Florida's Treasure Coast, is defined by its stunning barrier island and the vibrant Indian River Lagoon that separates it from the mainland. Connecting these two distinct halves are three crucial bridges, each playing a vital role in daily life, commerce, and recreation. For residents, visitors, and especially mariners, understanding the intricacies of these structures – their clearance heights, drawbridge schedules, and operational nuances – is key to a smooth and enjoyable experience.

This comprehensive guide from VeroTides.com delves into the specifics of the Barber Bridge (SR-60), the Wabasso Bridge (SR-510), and the 17th Street Bridge (SR-656). We'll cover everything from drawbridge opening times and vertical clearances to traffic tips and the ongoing rehabilitation project affecting the 17th Street Bridge. Whether you're a seasoned boater, an angler seeking the perfect spot, or simply curious about our local infrastructure, consider this your definitive resource.

### Understanding Drawbridge Operations: The Mariner's Basics

Before we dive into each specific bridge, let's establish some fundamental rules and best practices for interacting with drawbridges on the Indian River Lagoon. Safety and courtesy are paramount for both marine and vehicular traffic.

*   **VHF Radio:** Always monitor VHF Channel 16 and hail the bridge tender on Channel 13 when approaching a drawbridge. State your vessel's name, direction of travel, and request an opening.
*   **Sound Signals:**
    *   **One long blast followed by one short blast:** Request for opening.
    *   **Five short blasts:** Danger signal (used if the bridge tender doesn't respond or if there's an emergency).
*   **Bridge Tender Signals:**
    *   **One long blast:** Bridge will open.
    *   **Five short blasts:** Bridge will not open (due to schedule, malfunction, or emergency).
    *   **Red light:** Bridge is closed.
    *   **Green light:** Bridge is open or opening.
*   **No Wake Zones:** All bridges are typically surrounded by "No Wake" zones. Observe these strictly to prevent damage to other vessels, docks, and the sensitive shoreline, and to ensure safety for all.
*   **Currents:** Be acutely aware of strong currents, especially near Sebastian Inlet and during tidal changes, which can significantly impact your vessel's maneuverability when approaching a bridge.
*   **Patience:** Bridge tenders manage multiple requests and vehicular traffic. Be patient and maintain a safe distance from the bridge and other vessels while waiting.

### The Barber Bridge (SR-60 / Merrill P. Barber Bridge)

The Merrill P. Barber Bridge, commonly known as the Barber Bridge, is the iconic gateway to Vero Beach's barrier island, connecting the mainland via State Road 60 (20th Street) directly to Ocean Drive and the heart of our beachside community.

*   **Location:** Central Vero Beach, connecting 20th Street (mainland) to Beachland Road (barrier island).
    *   **Approximate Coordinates:** 27.6405° N, 80.3660° W
*   **Type:** High-level fixed span bridge. This is excellent news for mariners, as it means **no drawbridge operations!** You can pass underneath at any time, provided your vessel's height is within the clearance limits.
*   **Vertical Clearance:** The Barber Bridge offers a generous vertical clearance of **65 feet (19.8 meters)** at mean high water (MHW). This clearance accommodates most sailboats and larger vessels navigating the Indian River Lagoon.
*   **Significance:** Beyond its practical function, the Barber Bridge is a landmark. Its graceful arch provides stunning panoramic views of the lagoon, the city skyline, and the Atlantic Ocean. It's a popular spot for walkers, joggers, and cyclists.
*   **Boating & Fishing Tips:**
    *   **Navigation:** Due to its high clearance, navigation under the Barber Bridge is generally straightforward. Stay within the marked channel.
    *   **Currents:** While not as strong as near Sebastian Inlet, currents can still be noticeable, especially during strong tides.
    *   **Fishing:** The bridge pilings and surrounding structure can attract baitfish and larger predators. Anglers often target snook, redfish, and trout around the bridge. Be mindful of boat traffic and FWC regulations.
    *   **No Wake Zone:** Strictly observe the no-wake zone extending well beyond the bridge structure.

### The Wabasso Bridge (SR-510)

Heading north from central Vero Beach, the Wabasso Bridge serves as a vital link for the northern communities, connecting the mainland town of Wabasso to the barrier island, providing access to areas like Wabasso Beach Park and the northern reaches of the Indian River Lagoon.

*   **Location:** Northern Indian River County, connecting SR-510 (Wabasso Road) on the mainland to the barrier island.
    *   **Approximate Coordinates:** 27.7667° N, 80.4417° W
*   **Type:** Drawbridge (bascule bridge). This bridge opens to allow taller vessels to pass.
*   **Vertical Clearance (Closed):** The Wabasso Bridge provides a vertical clearance of approximately **21 feet (6.4 meters)** at MHW when closed. Vessels taller than this will require an opening.
*   **Drawbridge Schedule & Operations:**
    *   The Wabasso Bridge operates on demand, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.
    *   **Restrictions:** However, to ease vehicular traffic during peak commuting hours, the bridge **will not open for marine traffic** during the following times, Monday through Friday (excluding federal holidays):
        *   **7:30 AM to 8:30 AM**
        *   **4:30 PM to 5:30 PM**
    *   **Hailing the Tender:** Use VHF Channel 13 (working channel) or Channel 16 (hailing channel) to request an opening.
*   **Traffic Tips (Vehicular):** Expect delays during drawbridge openings. Plan your travel accordingly, especially during weekends or peak season.
*   **Boating & Fishing Tips:**
    *   **Approach:** Approach the Wabasso Bridge cautiously, especially if you require an opening. Maintain a safe distance from the bridge and other vessels.
    *   **Currents:** Proximity to Sebastian Inlet means currents can be stronger here than further south. Be prepared for swift water movement, particularly during outgoing tides.
    *   **Spoil Islands:** Several popular spoil islands are located north and south of the Wabasso Bridge, offering great spots for picnicking, fishing, and birdwatching.
    *   **Fishing:** The bridge structure and nearby channels are productive fishing grounds for snook, redfish, and various other species.

### The 17th Street Bridge (SR-656 / Alma Lee Loy Bridge)

The Alma Lee Loy Bridge, commonly known as the 17th Street Bridge, is the southernmost of Vero Beach's three main crossings, connecting the mainland via 17th Street to the barrier island, providing access to areas like South Beach Park and the southern Indian River Lagoon.

*   **Location:** Southern Vero Beach, connecting 17th Street (mainland) to the barrier island.
    *   **Approximate Coordinates:** 27.6200° N, 80.3550° W
*   **Type:** Drawbridge (bascule bridge). Similar to the Wabasso Bridge, it opens for taller vessels.
*   **Vertical Clearance (Closed):** The 17th Street Bridge offers a vertical clearance of approximately **21 feet (6.4 meters)** at MHW when closed. Vessels exceeding this height will need an opening.
*   **Drawbridge Schedule & Operations:**
    *   The 17th Street Bridge operates on demand, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.
    *   **Restrictions:** Similar to the Wabasso Bridge, to alleviate vehicular traffic congestion, the bridge **will not open for marine traffic** during the following times, Monday through Friday (excluding federal holidays):
        *   **7:30 AM to 8:30 AM**
        *   **4:30 PM to 5:30 PM**
    *   **Hailing the Tender:** Use VHF Channel 13 (working channel) or Channel 16 (hailing channel) to request an opening.

#### Current Rehabilitation Project: What Mariners and Drivers Need to Know

As of our publication date (2026-06-06), the 17th Street Bridge is undergoing a significant rehabilitation project aimed at extending its service life, enhancing safety, and improving operational efficiency. This project involves comprehensive structural repairs, mechanical and electrical system upgrades, and deck resurfacing.

*   **Project Scope:** The rehabilitation includes:
    *   Repairing concrete components on the bridge deck and substructure.
    *   Replacing key mechanical and electrical components of the drawbridge machinery.
    *   Upgrading the control system and operator's house.
    *   Resurfacing the bridge deck for a smoother ride.
*   **Impact on Vehicular Traffic:** Expect periodic lane closures, particularly during off-peak hours or overnight. While efforts are made to minimize disruption, drivers should anticipate potential delays and consider alternative routes (like the Barber Bridge) when possible. Signage will be clearly posted.
*   **Impact on Marine Traffic:**
    *   **Temporary Closures:** There may be instances of temporary, scheduled full closures of the drawbridge to marine traffic for specific work phases (e.g., machinery replacement). These closures are typically announced well in advance by the Coast Guard via Local Notice to Mariners (LNM).
    *   **Reduced Clearance:** During certain phases, scaffolding or temporary structures might slightly reduce the available vertical clearance, even when the bridge is closed. Always check current LNMs or contact the bridge tender for the latest information.
    *   **Construction Zones:** Mariners should exercise extreme caution when transiting near the bridge, observing any temporary buoys, barges, or work vessels in the area. Maintain a safe distance from construction equipment.
*   **Project Duration:** While specific timelines can shift, the project is anticipated to continue for several more months. Always refer to official Florida Department of Transportation (FDOT) updates or local news sources for the most current information.

### General Tips for Navigating Vero Beach Bridges and the Indian River Lagoon

*   **Always Consult Current Charts:** NOAA nautical charts are your best friend. They provide up-to-date information on depths, channels, aids to navigation, and bridge clearances.
*   **Monitor Weather:** The weather in Florida can change rapidly. Always check forecasts before heading out and be prepared for sudden storms.
*   **Observe FWC Regulations:** Familiarize yourself with Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission (FWC) regulations regarding fishing, boating, and manatee protection zones. The Indian River Lagoon is a critical habitat for manatees, and "Slow Speed" or "Minimum Wake" zones are strictly enforced.
*   **Enjoy the Spoil Islands:** The Indian River Lagoon is dotted with numerous spoil islands, many of which are publicly accessible and offer fantastic opportunities for picnicking, camping, or simply enjoying nature. Be sure to practice Leave No Trace principles.
*   **Safety First:** Always ensure you have all required safety equipment on board (life jackets, fire extinguisher, sound-producing device, etc.). File a float plan with someone on shore.
